Day 6. Cut-offs, two-cut-off designs and the intermediate zone

Tags: [S] primary public source, [Y] my synthesis, [D] derived by me by arithmetic from a cited count. The lab uses simulated data only.

(a) Concept

Idea Meaning
Single cut-off one threshold; Youden (max Se+Sp−1), a targeted Se or Sp, or cost-weighted
Two cut-offs rule-out cut-off set for sensitivity/NPV; rule-in cut-off set for specificity/PPV
Intermediate (“grey”) zone results between the two; the price paid for high predictive values
Lock before validation derive on one cohort, freeze, validate on another; never re-tune on validation data
Spectrum and prevalence the same cut-offs give different zone fractions and PPV/NPV in primary vs specialty care

Grey-zone approach: Coste & Pouchot, Int J Epidemiol 2003;32:304-313 [S: abstract]: a three-zone partition whose width depends on the separation of the group means, variability, and the accepted misclassification risks. TG-ROC plots sensitivity and specificity against the threshold so the zone limits can be read off.

Our Wilson intervals reproduce FDA’s PPA and NPA CIs to the printed digit (70.8-79.5, 69.4-76.6), so Wilson is the likely method [D: inference, not stated by FDA]. The PV CIs differ slightly because FDA derives them from likelihood-ratio CIs.

The PPA/NPA are low (≈75%) because intermediates count as misses (denominators include all PET+ and PET−). The headline strength is in the categories: PV 88.5% for positive, 95.3% NPV for negative, 19.9% (191/958) intermediate ✔. Derived by me [D] from the counts above (no CI in the FDA summary):

Also FDA’s decision logic is worth noting: the PV is judged “statistically higher than prevalence” (lower CI bound 84.9% > prevalence 39.5%), a comparison with the prevalence rather than a pre-specified performance goal; none of a sample-size calculation or goal was found in the text read. ✔ (FDA-DS p.27)

Known blemishes in the summary (do not copy tables uncritically) ✔: p.26 says “pre-specified cut-offs of 0.225 pg/mL” while the table uses 0.223, and “prevalence 13.1%” in one sentence vs 39.5% elsewhere; others are listed in the notes.

What the two papers do and do not give. Both use the prototype assay, not the cleared one, and neither reports 0.223/0.378. Hibar et al., Alzheimers Dement 2026;22(1):e71009: five cohorts, N = 2,148, single rule-out cut-off 0.189 pg/mL, no intermediate zone; e.g. cognitively impaired (n = 538) sensitivity 98.98%, specificity 29.17%, NPV 92.51% [S]. Hortsch et al., J Prev Alzheimers Dis 2026;13(5):100534: APPROACH, N = 588, centiloid reference, exploratory double cut-off at 90% PPA / 90% NPA, indeterminate 5.78% at centiloid 30, Wilson CIs, eGFR distribution reported and “higher pTau217 with worse kidney function” [S]. Nothing here is the pivotal data behind the clearance.

(c) R lab (simulated)

Cohorts

pTau217-like: log10 concentration normal in both groups, higher in amyloid-positive.

sim_cohort <- function(n, prev, seed, egfr_shift = FALSE) {
  set.seed(seed)
  y <- rbinom(n, 1, prev)
  lx <- ifelse(y == 1, rnorm(n, -0.20, 0.30), rnorm(n, -0.80, 0.25))
  egfr <- round(pmin(120, pmax(8, rnorm(n, 78, 22))))
  if (egfr_shift) lx <- lx + ifelse(egfr >= 60, 0, ifelse(egfr >= 30, 0.10, 0.30))  # assumed effect
  data.frame(y, x = 10^lx, egfr)
}
train <- sim_cohort(700, 0.40, 61)      # derivation cohort

How to state the claim when a result can be indeterminate (the FDA summary’s practice [S]): report the fraction of intermediates, performance of each category, and PPA/NPA with intermediates counted as not-agreeing; the “excluding intermediates” figures alone flatter the test. [Y]

Kidney function as a covariate (assumed effect)

The FDA summary has no eGFR-stratified analysis (only a kidney-disease comorbidity subgroup of N = 24) [S]. Hortsch et al. report higher pTau217 with worse kidney function in their prototype data [S]. Here: an assumed upward shift of the log marker in amyloid-negative and positive subjects with lower eGFR (+0.10 log10 for eGFR 30-59, +0.30 below 30); cut-offs stay locked.

kv <- sim_cohort(40000, 0.40, 64, egfr_shift = TRUE)

(d) Ten interview questions

show_questions(6, "../interview_questions.csv")

Q1. Describe the Elecsys pTau217 cleared cut-off scheme and what it reported.

FDA K261686 (decision 2026-08-19): negative <= 0.223, intermediate > 0.223 to <= 0.378, positive > 0.378 pg/mL. Pivotal N = 958 vs amyloid PET visual read (6 blinded readers): PV of positive 88.5% (285/322), NPV of negative 95.3% (424/445), intermediate 19.9% (191/958); PPA 75.4% and NPA 73.1% with intermediates counted as misses. Indication: aid in identifying amyloid pathology, age >= 55 with cognitive decline, with other clinical information.

Q2. <98><85> Why is the PPA only 75% if the test is called good?

Intermediates count as non-agreement: 72 of 378 PET+ fall in the intermediate zone and 21 in the negative zone. Excluding the 19.9% intermediates would flatter the numbers. The real information is in the category-specific predictive values and likelihood ratios (LR+ 11.8 for positive, 0.08 for negative).

Q3. What determines the width of the intermediate zone?

Separation of the group distributions (AUC), variability of the marker (biological and analytical), and the target performance for the two steps. Better separation or looser targets narrow it. It also depends on the population spectrum, not just the assay (Coste and Pouchot 2003).

Q4. <98><85> How would you derive two cut-offs, and why lock them?

On a derivation cohort: rule-out cut-off = highest value keeping sensitivity >= target; rule-in cut-off = lowest value keeping specificity >= target. Freeze them in the plan and evaluate once on an independent validation cohort, otherwise performance is optimistic (selection bias on the same data).

Q5. What did the FDA summary say about how 0.223 and 0.378 were chosen?

They were derived on 687 banked samples (286 PET+), but the target sensitivity/specificity or selection rule is not stated in the summary text I read (not verified). In an interview say what the public data show and that the rule is not disclosed.

Q6. How do you state performance when results can be indeterminate?

Report the indeterminate fraction, performance of each category (PV, LR), and PPA/NPA both with indeterminates counted as misses and, if useful, excluding them, being clear which is which. FDA’s summary reports the intermediate row explicitly and counts it as non-agreement in PPA/NPA.

Q7. Same cut-offs, primary vs specialist care - what changes?

Sensitivity and specificity per class are roughly constant (given the same spectrum), but PPV falls and NPV rises as prevalence falls; the zone fractions shift with spectrum. FDA reports PV of positive 78.3% in primary care vs 90.2% in secondary/tertiary, and a footnote about lower PPV in low-prevalence settings.

Q8. <98><85> How does kidney function affect plasma pTau217, and what does the FDA data show?

Prototype-assay data (Hortsch et al. 2026) show higher pTau217 with worse kidney function, with false positives in advanced CKD. The FDA decision summary has no eGFR-stratified analysis, only a kidney-disease comorbidity subgroup of N = 24, too small to conclude. A pre-specified eGFR subgroup analysis or eGFR-aware reporting would be a sensible plan item.

Q9. How do Hibar 2026 and Hortsch 2026 relate to the cleared test?

Both use a prototype assay and do not report the cleared cut-offs. Hibar: five cohorts, N = 2,148, single rule-out cut-off 0.189 pg/mL, no intermediate zone. Hortsch: N = 588, centiloid reference, exploratory 90%/90% double cut-off, 5.78% indeterminate at CL 30. They are supportive evidence, not the pivotal data.

Q10. Is monitoring a claim for the cleared test?

Roche’s 24 Aug 2026 release states it is not intended as a stand-alone test and that safety and effectiveness are not established for predicting dementia or for monitoring therapy. This appears in the release, not in the decision summary text I read; I have not seen the labelling.
